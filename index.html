Tokyo · October 5

Tokyo, Monday, October 5.

Monday the 5th starts at 5:30 in the morning at the tuna auction, so the morning is already spoken for. You sleep from about half past eight, come back for lunch at noon, and the afternoon starts after that. Two choices below: a place for lunch, and the afternoon that goes with it — or say you’d rather rest. Tuesday the 6th comes after that, once I know what time you want to start.

Monday, October 5 — lunch at noon Pick one. All five are open for lunch on Monday the 5th and take bookings. Each one is the quiet kind, a basement, an upper floor or a back lane, and each lunch is a short course, nowhere near 25 pieces. All five are in or next to Ginza.

The smallest option is written on each card. Prices are per person and were checked on each restaurant’s own page on September 27. Travel times are from your hotel.

1 · Higashi-Ginza, behind the Kabuki-za · sushiGinza SushimasaSushi again, but a short one. A basement room on the back side of the Kabuki-za theatre, so the street outside is quiet even at noon. The lunch is a short kaiseki: a few cooked dishes first, then a small round of nigiri, then dessert.

Iro course: 5 small dishes and 7 pieces of nigiri, ¥9,900. Yui course: 8 dishes and 9 pieces, ¥13,200. If you only want nigiri, the Ajisai lunch is 7 pieces with a small bowl of chirashi, ¥4,950.

About 15 min on foot, 5 by taxi. Monday lunch 11:30–14:30. Closed Wednesdays. Booking online or by phone. I book it. Afternoon: course G or F.

2 · Ginza 5-chome, 9th floor · tempuraTempura KondoTwo Michelin stars, on the ninth floor of a narrow building with almost no sign at street level. You sit at the counter and each piece is fried in front of you and put on your plate one at a time. Vegetables are the speciality here, not just seafood.

Sumire lunch: 9 pieces (2 prawn, 3 fish, 4 vegetable) with rice, pickles, red miso soup and fruit, ¥13,200. The next size up is 11 pieces, ¥16,500. Nothing bigger at lunch.

About 10 min on foot. Monday lunch in two seatings, 12:00 or 13:30. Closed Sundays. Booking by phone or online. I book the 12:00 seating. Afternoon: course G or F.

3 · Ginza 2-chome, basement · seasonal JapaneseGinza UchiyamaA basement room on a side street at the quiet end of Ginza, booking only, 25 seats with 9 at the counter and two private rooms. The house dish is sea bream over rice with hot tea poured on at the end. The lunch is small plates, one after another, and ends with that.

Lunch course: 7 dishes ending with the sea-bream rice, ¥5,500 with tax and service. An 8-dish version with better ingredients is a little more.

About 20 min on foot, 7 by taxi. Monday lunch 11:30–14:30. Closed on public holidays only (the 5th is not one). Booking online. I book it. Afternoon: course G or F.

4 · Nihonbashi, 3 min from Tokyo Station · kappoNihonbashi YukariA family restaurant since 1935, now run by the third generation, Kimio Nonaga, who won the Iron Chef title in 2002. Old-style Tokyo cooking, plainly presented. There are private rooms in the basement with sunken tables, so you can have the room to yourselves.

Lunch is a set tray, the Yukari gozen, about ¥4,000, booked the day before. Courses above that on request. All of it is modest in size.

About 10 min by taxi. Monday lunch 11:30–14:00, last order 13:30. Closed Sundays and holidays. Phone booking. I call and book a private room. Afternoon: course G or F.

5 · Higashi-Ginza, Kobikicho · eel and JapaneseChikuyotei, main houseAn eel house from the 1860s, still in a wooden building with a small garden, a few streets behind the Kabuki-za. The tatami rooms are booked as private rooms for two or more and serve a set course; the grilled eel comes in the middle of it, not as a giant bowl.

Lunch course in a private tatami room: 7 to 8 small dishes with the eel as the main, ¥10,230 plus 10% service. If you would rather sit at a table, the eel bowl is ¥3,630.

About 15 min on foot, 5 by taxi. Monday lunch 11:30–15:30, last order 14:30. Closed Sundays and holidays. Phone booking for the room. I call and book it. Afternoon: course G or F.

Monday, October 5 — the afternoon Either afternoon goes with any lunch. Course G stays in Ginza on foot; course F is one direct train to Kappabashi, the kitchen street, and the old temple beside it. Course G · Stationery, a food hall, a rooftop garden, tea · $250 · about four hours

Ginza, on foot from the table

This goes with any of the five lunches. You stand up from the table and the afternoon is already around you: everything below is inside one neighbourhood, flat, and about two kilometres end to end, with a seat every forty minutes. It is the shopping afternoon — paper, pens, a food hall, sweets to carry back — with a garden on a roof in the middle of it. From lunch 4 in Nihonbashi it starts with a ten-minute taxi; from the others you walk.

Guide fee $250 · about four hours. Entry tickets, trains, taxis and meals are settled on the day as they come.

The day

  1. 13:30
    Itoya, Ginza 2-chomeTwelve floors of paper and pens in one narrow building, since 1904. Letter sets, washi paper, fountain pens, wrapping — gifts that pack flat. There is a café on the top floor if you want to sit before you start.
  2. 14:30
    Mitsukoshi, the basement food hallFive minutes down the main street. Two floors under the department store of wagashi, tea, pickles, rice crackers and bento from every part of Japan, most of it boxed to travel. The easiest place in Tokyo to buy food gifts, and I translate the counters.
  3. 15:15
    Kimuraya, across the crossingThe bakery that invented the sweet-bean bun in 1874, on the corner of the Ginza crossing under the clock tower. A paper bag of five for the hotel room.
  4. 15:30
    Ginza Six, the roofA department store with a garden on top, free, open to anyone. Benches, trees, and the whole of Ginza below you. Then the art bookshop on the sixth floor, which is worth a look even if you buy nothing.
  5. 16:20
    Tea at Nakamura TokichiOn the fourth floor of the same building: a tea house from Uji, near Kyoto, that has been selling matcha since 1854. A bowl of tea, or a matcha jelly and ice cream, sitting down. This is the rest before the walk home.
  6. 16:50
    Takumi, the folk-craft shopSeven minutes on, in Ginza 8-chome, a small shop that has sold Japanese folk craft since 1933: hand-made ceramics, textiles, and the wooden kokeshi dolls and small painted toys from country workshops. If the little dolls Linda bought before are what you like, this is the Ginza shop for them. Open Monday 11:00–19:00.
  7. 17:15
    Back at the hotelAbout eight minutes on foot from Takumi, or a five-minute taxi.

The route

Lunch → Itoya on foot (5–15 min, or a 10-min taxi from Nihonbashi). Itoya → Mitsukoshi 5 min. Mitsukoshi → Kimuraya across the crossing. Kimuraya → Ginza Six 5 min. Ginza Six → Takumi 7 min. Takumi → hotel about 8 min on foot or a short taxi. Open the route in Google Maps ↗

Course F · Kitchen street, food samples, Senso-ji · $250 · about four hours

Kappabashi, and the old temple next door

This goes with any of the five lunches. Kappabashi is Tokyo's restaurant-supply street — knives, tableware, and the workshops that make the plastic food models you have seen in every restaurant window. From lunch it is one direct train, no changes. You make a food sample of your own first, walk the street, then Tokyo’s oldest temple ten minutes away on foot with a three-hundred-year-old street of stalls in front of it, and a taxi home.

Guide fee $250 · about four hours. Entry tickets, trains, taxis and meals are settled on the day as they come.

The day

  1. 13:30
    One train from lunchThe Ginza line from Ginza or Nihonbashi station straight to Tawaramachi, about 20 minutes, sitting down. Lunch 1 and 5 are a five-minute walk to Ginza station; lunch 4 is on top of Nihonbashi station.
  2. 14:15
    Make your own food sampleAt Ganso Shokuhin Sample-ya, the shop that popularised the craft in 1932. A booked 40-minute session making a piece of wax tempura or a lettuce leaf, which you keep. I book the Monday slot in advance.
  3. 15:00
    Kappabashi Kitchen StreetA few hundred metres of restaurant-supply shops: knives that can be engraved with your name while you wait, lacquerware, ceramics by the piece, and the original food-model shops. Most shops are open Mondays — it is Sundays that are quiet here.
  4. 15:45
    Senso-ji, on footTen minutes from the street. Tokyo’s oldest temple, founded in 628: in under the five-metre paper lantern, along Nakamise — a street of rice-cracker and fan stalls that has been a shopping street for three hundred years — to the main hall. Near the temple end is Sukeroku, a shop the size of a cupboard that has sold tiny hand-made Edo toys and dolls since 1866, for anyone who likes the small kokeshi kind of thing. Flat, with benches in the courtyard.
  5. 16:30
    Something sweet on NakamiseMelon bread out of the oven, or a bag of hot senbei, eaten as we walk back to the gate.
  6. 17:00
    Taxi to the hotelAbout 25 minutes across the city, sitting down.

The route

Lunch → Ginza or Nihonbashi station on foot, then the Ginza line to Tawaramachi, about 20 min. Tawaramachi → Ganso Sample-ya 12 min on foot. On foot along Kappabashi. Kappabashi → Senso-ji 10 min on foot. Asakusa → hotel about 25 min by taxi. Open the route in Google Maps ↗

What it costs The same basis on the 6th too.

US$250 a day — or ¥40,000 in cash — whichever course you pick.

Four hours or five, it is the same figure — so pick the one you actually want rather than the one that looks like less work.

The fee covers my time only, set up the way we agreed: entry tickets, trains, taxis and meals are settled on the day as they come. Dollars by payment link, like the kabuki tickets, or yen in cash on the day — whichever is easier for you. Nothing is owed if you wake up and decide you would rather not.

And one honest word.

If you wake up on the 5th, do the auction, sleep, and then decide that the afternoon is more than you want, say so and we cancel it. Nothing is owed. That is a good answer and I would rather have it than watch you push through.

Reply to Yuuki with a lunch number and a letter for the 5th, plus a start time for the 6th — or with “none, we will rest”, which is a real answer and not a disappointing one. Times are approximate and can move earlier or later on the day.
