Tokyo: Tuesday, October 6

Temples, shopping, and the busiest crossing on Earth.

  • Start: 9:00 a.m. (hotel pickup)
  • End time: to confirm with you
  • Distance: train and walk

The day

  1. 9:00
    Hotel pickup Yuuki will meet you at The Blossom Hibiya. Bring comfortable shoes for walking.
  2. 9:30–11:00
    Meiji Jingu Shrine A forest shrine in the heart of the city. Walking paths are quiet and shaded. No admission fee. The shrine is dedicated to the Emperor Meiji and has been a place of worship since 1920. Bring coins if you want to offer a small prayer (¥100 or ¥500). Bathrooms available.
  3. 11:15 a.m.–12:30 p.m.
    Harajuku and Omotesando walk A 15-minute walk through Tokyo's teen fashion district (Harajuku) into the high-end shopping street (Omotesando, which locals call "Tokyo's Champs-Élysées"). Shops, cafes, trees, people-watching. Stop at a small café if you want coffee.
  4. 12:30–1:45 p.m.
    Lunch (60–75 minutes) Yuuki will choose a restaurant based on the day. Options include casual ramen, udon, or a small set lunch near Omotesando or Meiji-Jingumae station. Budget: ¥1,200–3,000.
  5. 2:00–3:00 p.m.
    Shibuya Crossing The world's busiest pedestrian crossing. Stand above it from a café or shop window, or walk across it yourself. The crossing is about 500 meters (1,600 feet) from lunch. You will see thousands of people move like a choreographed flow. A few minutes of photos, then move on.
  6. 3:00 p.m. onward
    Optional: Kappabashi Kitchen Street If you have energy, Kappabashi (kitchen equipment and restaurant supply street) is one Ginza Line train ride away, no transfer needed. Row of shops selling beautiful knives, ceramics, cooking tools, and restaurant supplies for home cooks. Most shops close around 5:00 p.m., so go soon if interested. About 1–2 hours if you browse.
    Kappabashi is practical, not touristy. If you are tired, skip it. The day will be full.
